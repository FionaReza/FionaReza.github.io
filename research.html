01

Growth conditions of Alexandrium monilatum

2024–2026

I study how environmental conditions affect the growth and bloom potential of Alexandrium monilatum, a harmful dinoflagellate isolated from the York River. I conducted five replicated culture experiments, measuring growth through fluorescence, FlowCam and microscope cell counts, and chlorophyll-a analysis. The strain grew best near 17 ppt and generally at 25°C rather than 20°C or 29°C, but did not sustain growth at 10 or 36 ppt.

In another set of treatments, cultures at 25 ppt reached about 3,050 cells mL−1 at 24°C, compared with 330 at 20°C and 1,550 at 28°C. At 24°C, peak abundance at 25 ppt was about three times that at 30 ppt and nine times that at 36 ppt. At 28°C, the highest density occurred at 20 ppt, suggesting that temperature and salinity interact. A separate light treatment reached about 4,000 cells mL−1, supporting further experiments on light intensity. My broader work also examines nitrogen sources and feeding on cryptophyte prey.

To compare these results with natural blooms, I independently compiled Chesapeake Bay bloom and non-bloom records from monitoring agencies and published studies for 1986–2024. During 2013–2017, recorded blooms occurred at 24.7–30°C and 17.6–23.87 ppt, with 1,090–4,720 cells mL−1. Comparing these records with studies of growth, nutrients, and microbial community changes highlighted gaps in our understanding of local strains and helped guide my experiments on conditions that favor Chesapeake Bay blooms.

02

Seasonal shifts of phytoplankton assemblages in the Lafayette River

Summers 2025 & 2026

Examining seasonal phytoplankton shifts and bloom dynamics using weekly to high-frequency observations linked to environmental and nutrient variability. Field measurements include temperature, salinity, dissolved oxygen, tidal depth, nutrients, chlorophyll a, particulate nitrogen, and particulate carbon. I contribute to bloom monitoring, identify and image phytoplankton, and upload image files to EcoTaxa to support image-based taxonomic annotation and community analysis. FlowCam is used to quantify major plankton groups.

03

Extraction and characterization of sodium alginate from brown seaweeds

2021–2022

As part of a broader project exploring chitosan–sodium alginate formulations for plant growth regulation and produce preservation, I extracted and characterized sodium alginate from five brown seaweed species collected from St. Martin’s Island, Bangladesh. I compared calcium chloride and formaldehyde-based extraction methods, assessed seaweed composition, and characterized the extracted alginate using FTIR, fluorescence spectroscopy, X-ray diffraction, intrinsic viscosity, and molecular-weight analysis. I also purified samples for NMR spectroscopy.

Sargassum binderi produced the highest alginate yield (33.5 ± 1.5%), while Padina tetrastromatica had the highest intrinsic viscosity. These findings highlighted their potential as local raw materials for food and textile applications, with lower-viscosity alginates offering possibilities for drug-delivery formulations.

04

Microplastic abundance and distribution in the Naf River Estuary

2021–2022

I contributed to a preliminary investigation of microplastic pollution in surface water and bottom sediment of Bangladesh’s Naf River Estuary. The study examined particle abundance, polymer composition, size, shape, and color to establish a baseline for future monitoring and pollution management. Mean abundance was 25.6 particles/kg in water and 186 particles/kg dry weight in sediment. Fibers and particles smaller than 1 mm predominated, with polyethylene most common in water and polypropylene in sediment.

A coauthored manuscript is under review at the Journal of Coastal Conservation.

05

Plankton diversity around Kutubdia Island

2020

I contributed to a study of plankton diversity and abundance in relation to biophysical conditions around Kutubdia Island on Bangladesh’s east coast. Surface-water sampling covered eight stations representing high- and low-tide conditions. The study used Motic imaging software for plankton identification and SPSS to examine relationships between plankton abundance and environmental variables.

Surveys identified 82 plankton species: 55 phytoplankton and 27 zooplankton. The phytoplankton assemblage comprised 44 diatom and 11 dinoflagellate species; no coccolithophores were recorded. Common diatom genera included Coscinodiscus, Thalassiosira, Chaetoceros, and Rhizosolenia. Phytoplankton diversity was highest in winter, while abundance ranged from approximately 1,085–10,985 cells L−1 in winter and 2,199–129,310 cells L−1 in summer.

Plankton abundance was significantly correlated with sea-surface temperature, pH, salinity, and dissolved oxygen. Across observations, temperature ranged from 27.3–31.7 °C, salinity from 24–33 ppt, pH from 5.9–8.4, and dissolved oxygen from 6.69–10.81 mg L−1. These findings highlighted seasonal variation in coastal plankton communities and their relationships with local water conditions.

06

Land-cover and shoreline changes on St. Martin’s Island

February–March 2020

During my internship at the Bangladesh Space Research and Remote Sensing Organization (SPARRSO) in Agargaon, Dhaka, I assessed land-use, land-cover, and shoreline changes on St. Martin’s Island, Bangladesh. I used ERDAS IMAGINE and ArcGIS for raster processing, spatial analysis, and mapping of 30 m Landsat imagery from 1989, 2000, 2010, and 2020.

The analysis indicated vegetation loss exceeding 30 hectares, expansion of land-use categories to more than 35 hectares, and substantial shoreline change. The report also identified more than 200 hectares of decline in areas classified as coral reef and shore, highlighting the value of remote sensing for monitoring coastal change.
