Monitoring and Event Response for Harmful Algal Blooms (MERHAB)

As part of MERHAB, a project supporting harmful algal bloom monitoring and forecasting in the lower Chesapeake Bay, I examined seasonal phytoplankton assemblages in the Lafayette River using water-quality monitoring and FlowCam analysis. Comparing January–June observations from 2025 and 2026 revealed marked seasonal and interannual differences in community composition. Dinoflagellate abundance reached approximately 320 cells mL−1 in June 2025 and 5,900 cells mL−1 in June 2026, while cryptomonad peaks were approximately 350 and 2,300 cells mL−1 in the respective years. These findings underscored the variability of phytoplankton communities and deepened my interest in how environmental fluctuations shape plankton succession and bloom dynamics.

Research responsibilities

  • Examined seasonal phytoplankton assemblages in the Lafayette River using water-quality observations and FlowCam analysis.
  • Identified and quantified major phytoplankton groups to characterize community composition.
  • Compared January–June observations from 2025 and 2026 to assess seasonal and interannual changes in abundance.
  • Interpreted community patterns in relation to environmental variability and bloom dynamics.

Lafayette River Water-Quality Monitoring to Support Total Maximum Daily Load (TMDL) Assessment

This project aims to improve the assessment of bloom-related water quality impairments in the Lower James River watershed by examining the daily vertical migration of Margalefidinium polykrikoides in the Lafayette River. Understanding how these movements affect the relationship between surface chlorophyll concentrations and dinoflagellate abundance throughout the water column can support more effective bloom detection and monitoring.

Research responsibilities

  • Supported weekly field sampling and measured water-column temperature, salinity, and chlorophyll using a YSI sonde.
  • Collected water samples for nutrient, ammonia, and particulate carbon and nitrogen analyses.
  • Collected samples for laboratory chlorophyll extraction and fluorometric analysis.
  • Collected plankton samples and processed whole-water samples using both FlowCam and PlanktoScope, uploading imaging runs to EcoTaxa to support taxonomic annotation.
  • Contributed water-quality and plankton imaging data to support the development of a bloom forecasting system.

Chitosan–Sodium Alginate Formulations for Plant Growth and Produce Preservation

This project investigates chitosan–sodium alginate formulations as low-cost products for plant growth regulation and the preservation of fruits and vegetables, with the broader aim of reducing reliance on chemical fertilizers.

My work focused on extracting and characterizing sodium alginate from brown seaweeds and evaluating their suitability as raw materials. Proximate analysis showed higher carbohydrate content in Sargassum binderi and higher ash content in Padina pavonica.

Research responsibilities

  • Collected brown seaweeds, extracted sodium alginate, and compared extraction yields.
  • Assessed seaweed composition through proximate analysis of moisture, ash, protein, fat, and carbohydrate content.
  • Characterized sodium alginate using fluorescence spectroscopy and X-ray diffraction (XRD).
  • Purified alginate samples for nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy.
  • Measured intrinsic viscosity and determined molecular weight.
