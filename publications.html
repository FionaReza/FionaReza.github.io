A preliminary approach to investigate the abundance, spatial distribution, and ecological risk of microplastics in surface water and sediment of the Naf River, Bangladesh

Islam, M. S., Ahmed, M. K., Chowdhury, K. M. A., Islam, M. S., Shampa, M. T. A., Reza, F., & Albin, E. Journal of Coastal Conservation. Manuscript under review.

Abstract

The understanding of the abundance and distribution of Microplastics (MPs) in the surface water and sediment of the Naf River Estuary remains incomplete, as some short-term studies have been conducted but no significant comprehensive studies are available. Therefore, this study investigated the distribution of MP pollution, and their morphological characteristics within the Naf River estuary, a major transportation route connecting Teknaf to Saint Martin’s Island. A total of five samples were collected for the analysis of MPs from both the surface water and bottom sediment. The average MP count in water samples was 25.6 particles/kg, while in sediment samples, it was 186 particles/kg of dry weight. The predominant polymer type observed in sediment samples is polypropylene (PP), constituting 37.23%, while in water samples, polyethylene (PE) dominated, comprising 37.5%. For sediment, the dominant polymer type is in the descending order of polypropylene (PP) > polyethylene (PE) > polystyrene (PS) > polyethylene terephthalate (PET) > polyurethane (PU) > polyvinyl chloride (PVC). On the other hand, for the water sample, the presence of polymer type followed the descending order of PE > PP > PS > PET > PU > PVC. The distribution of colors followed the sequence of white > colored > transparent in both sediment and water. The prevailing shape of MP for both sediment and water is fiber, accounting for 58.06% and 59.26%. In sediment samples, around 60.64% of MPs are categorized under the <1mm size class, Conversely, in water samples, the distribution is different, with approximately 81.25% of MPs falling under the <1mm size class. Therefore, this study shows the initial knowledge about the MP pollution and their morphological traits in the Naf River Estuary which will help to make any further sustainable pollution management strategies.

Production of sodium alginate from naturally occurring seaweeds in Bangladesh and its prospects in industrial biotechnology

View poster on ResearchGate ↗

Reza, F., Momtaz, N., et al. International Conference on Biotechnology in Sustainable Development. 2023

Abstract

Sodium Alginate (SA) found in the cell wall of brown seaweeds, linear polymer of β-D-mannuronic acid (M) and α-L-guluronic acid (G) linked by 1–4 bond has versatile use in food, textile and pharmaceutical industries. In this study, Sargassum binderi, Padina pavonica, Padina tetrastromatica, Hydroclathrus clathratus, and Dictyota dichotoma species were collected from St. Martin’s Island, Bangladesh. Calcium chloride and formaldehyde approach were used to extract SA from seaweeds. The maximum yield of SA was obtained from Sargassum binderi (33.5±1.5%).  The characteristics of the major functional groups of SA were measured by using FT-IR technology. Peaks at 3281, 1599, and 1408 cm−1 indicate the presence of hydroxyl (OH), carbonyl (C=O), and carboxyl (COOH) groups, respectively. SA’s ability to create viscous solution in water is one of the major properties for industrial applications such as texturizing food products, as well as in the textile printing, and dying. The intrinsic viscosity obtained from sodium alginates ranges from 35 to 111 cm3 g−1 where Padina tetrastromatica> Sargassum binderi> Dictyota dichotoma> Padina pavonica> Hydroclathrus clathratus. The molecular weight which was calculated ranges from 0.16 to 0.52×105 g mol−1. This is why, both Sargassum binderi and Padina tetrastromatica have greater economic value in the textile and food industries. Besides, low viscosities are helpful as well in obtaining polyelectrolyte complexes to produce drug delivery micro and nanoparticles. Therefore, the pharmaceutical industry can find SA’s application compelling.

Plankton diversity and abundance concerning biophysical conditions around Kutubdia Island, East Coast of Bangladesh

Abdullah, M., Ahmed, M. K., Khandakar, M. M., & Reza, F. 4th International Conference on Bangladesh Environment (ICBEN-4). 2020

Abstract

Kutubdia is an Island located in the Bay of Bengal is in threat of submerging due to climate change and sea level rise. The present study attempts to identify the plankton diversity and abundance in relation to biophysical conditions in the surface water of Kutubdia Island from November 2017 to March 2018. Samples were collected during summer and winter seasons from 8 stations (4 stations during high tide and 4 stations during low tide) around Kutubdia Island. Associated with the large volume of fresh water supply and interrelated oceanographic, biological and sedimentary processes driven by the monsoon winds of Bay of Bengal, species abundance shows significant variations in percentage contribution of individual species to total phytoplankton population in Monsoon and also in winter. Seasonal changes in abundance and diversity of phytoplankton significantly differed showing maximum diversity in winter and minimum in summer. Phytoplankton density ranged approximately from (1085 – 10985) cells L−1 in winter and (2199- 129310) cells L−1 in summer respectively. Fresh water from the rivers largely influences the coastal northern part of the Bay which involves variation of salinity (24-33parts per thousand) in surface water of Kutubdia Island. In the southern part of Kutubdia high wave action has been recorded. For the identification of cells, imaging software Motic and for showing correlation statistical software SPSS were used. Correlation studies of total cell count to physicochemical variables shows significant correlation with Sea Surface Temperature (SST), pH, salinity and dissolved Oxygen (DO) where in two seasons SST variation is detected from 27.3 °C to 31.7°C, pH ranges from 5.9 to 8.4 and DO is observed from 6.69 mg L−1 to 10.81 mg L−1.
